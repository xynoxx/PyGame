# Skyline

A 3D real-time strategy game (RTS) about holding out in a zombie apocalypse, made with **pygame** (window, input, 2D overlay) and **ModernGL** (3D drawing with OpenGL 3.3). Everything you see is made in code: no model, texture or sound files.

It's a playable prototype. Your HQ stands in the middle of the map. You train soldiers and engineers with supply, build turrets and bullet factories with metal and supply, strip junkyards and warehouses out on the map for more metal and supply, keep your guns in bullets, dig trenches, and hold off waves of zombies that grow every 30 seconds. Units take the orders of a classic RTS: queues, patrol, guard, repair, fire states and control groups. If the HQ falls, the game is over.

## Run it

```
pip install pygame moderngl
python Skyline/main.py              # full screen
python Skyline/main.py --window     # in a 1280 × 720 window
```

- **Python:** 3.10 or newer (built and tested with 3.12, pygame 2.6, ModernGL 5).
- **Graphics card:** needs OpenGL 3.3.
- **Full screen:** the game opens over the whole screen at the screen's own size, as a window with no border, so Alt+Tab to another program and back is quick. Esc pressed twice with nothing selected, or Alt+F4, quits. The panels and buttons keep their size in pixels, so on a big screen they take up less of it. With `--window` it plays in a 1280 × 720 window, as the tests and `--screenshot` always do.
- **Pictures without playing:** `python Skyline/main.py --screenshot out.png` plays a scripted game and saves pictures (see [Testing](#testing)).
- **Version and release:** this is version 0.1 (`main.VERSION`, in the window's title). `python Skyline/build_app.py` builds a Windows app that runs without Python (`dist/Skyline/Skyline.exe`) and zips it for itch.io (`pip install pillow pyinstaller` first); [itch/README.md](itch/README.md) has the steps to publish it and the page's text, with the cover and screenshots beside it. The packaged game writes any crash to `%APPDATA%\Skyline\crash.log`.

## How the game plays

1. **The HQ stands in the middle of the map.** The map is 600 by 600 tiles, three by three squares of 200; at the start only the middle square is open, the HQ in its middle, and the land all round it is darker. Nobody of yours can go out there, nothing can be built there, and the camera keeps to the open square, until a **radar station** is up (see 4): then the whole map is open, for good. The game opens looking at it, and the 60-second countdown to the first wave starts at once. The HQ is a dark four-storey block behind a low wall with barbed wire; it has 1,000 health and repairs itself (from nothing to whole in 10 minutes).
   - **Round timers:** over the HQ, a round timer for each material it makes, and over each building that's at work (a bullet factory, a salvage crane, a loading dock post), one for what it makes. Each is a ring in the material's colour that fills round as the next batch comes on, with the material's mark inside and the seconds left under it.
   - **Materials:** at the top left, what you have to spend: **supply** (to train troops), **metal** (to build turrets and buildings) and **bullets**. You start with 3,000 supply, 500 metal and 4,000 bullets. The HQ makes 1,000 supply, 100 metal and 500 bullets every 30 seconds, bullet factories make more bullets, and salvage cranes and loading dock posts strip junkyards and warehouses for more metal and supply (see 4). Every shot a soldier, a turret or a tank's machine gun fires uses a bullet (a tank's shell, 30), and with none left no gun or turret can fire: they still turn and aim, the bullets show red, and the game says so. Beside each material, as in Beyond All Reason, two small numbers: what's used of it every 30 seconds (red, over: the troops' upkeep, what the buildings at work use, what's being trained and built, and the bullets fired in the last 30 seconds) and what's made (green, under: by the HQ and the buildings at work). Hover the materials for what's made where, and what the troops eat; select the HQ or a building to see what it makes, under its buttons. The **+** beside them adds 10,000 supply, 5,000 metal and 10,000 bullets, for testing.
   - **Upkeep:** every 30 seconds each living soldier and engineer eats 50 supply from the stores, and each tank uses 100, the oldest first; a soldier or a tank uses 5 less for each level past the first. One there isn't enough supply for goes **hungry**: he walks 80% slower (at a fifth of his speed) and hits 30 times in 100 less at every range (80% becomes 50%), and a crate struck through in red shows beside his health bar; the game says when troops go hungry. He's fed (and back to himself) as soon as there's enough supply for him. Those still in training, and the dead, eat nothing.
   - **Pause:** the Pause key, or the button at the top right. Nothing moves while it's paused, but you can still look round, select, give orders, lay out turrets and trenches, and queue training; it all happens once the game goes on.
   - **Double speed:** the F key, or the ×2 button beside the pause button (lit gold while it's on). Everything in the game goes twice as fast, and plays out just as it would at normal speed; the camera doesn't speed up. Handy for testing.
2. **Train units.** Click the HQ. Its panel (bottom left) has **Soldier** (T) and **Engineer** (G) buttons, and under them what the HQ makes, each by its material's mark: +1000/30s supply, +100/30s metal, +500/30s bullets. A soldier takes 2 seconds and costs 50 supply a second while he trains (100 in all); an engineer takes 5 and costs 10 a second (50). A click puts one on the queue, Shift + click puts 5, and Ctrl + click 20; right click takes them off the same way. The keys work the same with Shift and Ctrl. Hover a button to see the unit's damage, range, health, upkeep, training time and cost.
   - **Paying as it goes** (as in Beyond All Reason): nothing is paid when a unit is queued or a turret or building is laid. The one in training is paid for a bit at a time as it trains, and a turret or building as it's built (faster with more engineers on it, the same in all). With too little of something for the next bit, the work waits, costing nothing, and goes on when there's enough: a building says "waiting for metal" (or supply) by its name, and a button is dimmed while there's none at all of something it takes. Taking the one in training off the queue gives back what's been paid for it. A blueprint never started cost nothing; a turret or building taken down gives nothing back.
   - New units walk to the rally flag; right click the ground with the HQ selected to move the flag.
3. **Command units:**
   - **Select:** left click a unit, or drag a box round several. Shift adds to the selection. Double click a unit to select every one of its kind on screen.
   - **Move:** right click the ground. They move in a square block.
   - **Line up:** hold the right button and drag along the ground. They stand in a line along it (in rows if it's short), facing away from where they came from.
   - **Attack:** right click a zombie to send the soldiers after it. Engineers carry no gun: they keep to what they were doing.
   - **Queue:** hold **Shift** with any order (a move, a line, an attack, a patrol, laying turrets or trenches) to have it done after the orders already given. Each selected unit's orders show as lines on the ground, in the order's colour.
   - **Patrol (P):** press P, then click the ground. They walk there and back, again and again; Shift-click more points to make a longer beat. Soldiers stop to shoot what comes in reach. Engineers on patrol build or repair any turret within 8 tiles of their beat, and soldiers dig any trench, then go back to their beat.
   - **Guard:** right click one of your own units, a turret or the HQ. They stay by it and go after any zombie that's going for it. A unit guarding one of his own kind helps with his work (building or digging), and an engineer guarding a turret repairs it when it's hurt.
   - **Repair:** right click a hurt turret with engineers selected (as fast as they'd build it). Anyone else selected guards it. The HQ repairs itself.
   - **Stop (H):** drop every order and stand.
   - **Remove (X, engineers only):** press X (or the Remove button), then click a turret or a building, built or still being built. The engineers take it down bit by bit, as long as it took to build; it's of no use from the first blow (a turret stops firing, a building stops making), and once it's gone its tiles are free. On a blueprint, it just calls it off.
   - **Fire state (L, soldiers):** fire at will (the start), return fire (only at zombies going for them), or hold fire. Sent after a zombie, they shoot it whatever the state. Engineers have no gun, and keep building with zombies about.
   - **Groups:** Ctrl + a number key makes the selection a group (a unit is in one group at a time). The number selects it again; press it twice to look at it. Shift + the number adds the group to the selection.
   - **Idle engineers (I):** while any engineer has no orders, an **Idle** button shows at the bottom right, with an engineer's portrait and how many there are. Click it (or press I) to select the next one, and the camera goes to him; each click goes on to the next, round them all. An engineer on guard or on patrol isn't idle.
   - The units panel has buttons for Patrol, Stop and the fire state (with soldiers among them), and for what the selected units can build. Hover a build button to see its numbers and cost.
4. **Build turrets.** Select engineers and press **B** for a **semi-auto turret** or **M** for a **full-auto turret** (a minigun; or click their buttons). A semi-auto turret takes one engineer 30 seconds and costs 50 metal every 10 seconds while it's built (150 in all), and has 100 health; a full-auto one takes 100 seconds and costs 100 metal every 10 seconds (1,000), has 500 health, and beats it in every way in a fight. Each engineer more on one turret builds a fifth as fast again as one (two take 25 seconds over a semi-auto turret, three 21), and pays as fast.
   - **Bullet factory (N):** a small brick works with two chimneys and two tanks, 4 by 3 tiles (the HQ is 5 by 4). It takes one engineer 30 seconds (each one more a fifth as fast again), costing 50 metal every 10 seconds while it's built (150 in all), has 200 health, and once built makes 200 bullets every 10 seconds for as long as it stands, each batch using up 40 supply and 10 metal from the stores. While there isn't that much, or the stores are full of bullets (they hold 20,000 at most: any more are lost, the HQ's and the test button's too), it waits, its batch standing still, and uses nothing; the game says when the bullets are full, and the number of them shows gold. Press N (or its button), then click where it goes: it's centred on the mouse, and needs all its tiles clear (green where it fits, red where it doesn't). With Shift, the button stays on for more. Like a turret, it's a blueprint until an engineer starts on it, and can be repaired or taken down. Select it to see its health, what it makes (+200/10s, by the bullet's mark) and, in red under that, what it uses (−40/10s supply, −10/10s metal); they're dim while it waits.
   - **Junkyards and warehouses:** the open square has four of each, at random spots: one of each 20 to 26 tiles from the HQ, one of each 30 to 40 out, and two of each 50 to 90 out, toward where the dead come in. A **junkyard** (8 by 8 tiles) is a yard of mismatched corrugated sheets with 50 wrecked cars in it, stacked five high, an old broken crusher and piles of tyres, and holds 5,000 metal. A **warehouse** (8 by 6) is a shed of ribbed steel with a loading dock and three roll-up doors along its front and pallets of stock on the dock, and holds 20,000 supply. Nobody can walk through either. Each faces the HQ, and beside its front is a spot 3 tiles square for the building that strips it. Click one to see what's left in it.
   - **Salvage crane (J):** a crane on tracks with a long jointed arm and a magnet, and a baler beside it. It takes one engineer 40 seconds, costing 100 supply and 50 metal every 10 seconds while it's built (400 supply and 200 metal in all), and has 400 health. Press J (or its button), then click a junkyard (or near its spot): it goes up on the spot, its arm reaching over the fence. Once built, every 30 seconds it swings out over the yard, lifts the top car off the nearest stack, swings it back and drops it in the baler: 100 metal each time (a junkyard lasts 25 minutes). The stacks go down from the front as it works.
   - **Loading dock post (K):** a box truck backed up to the warehouse's middle door, a forklift, sandbags round it and a floodlight. It costs what a salvage crane does, takes as long, and has 400 health. Press K, then click a warehouse. Once built, crates pile up by the truck, and every 10 seconds a load goes to the stores: 500 supply (a warehouse lasts under 7 minutes). The pallets on the dock go, and the doors roll up, as the warehouse is emptied.
   - **On and off:** a bullet factory, salvage crane or loading dock post has an **On/Off** switch at the top right of its panel (or press **O** with it selected). Switched off, its batch stands still and it uses nothing: a bullet factory takes no supply or metal, a crane rests over its baler. Its round timer goes grey with a pause sign, its name says "(off)", and what's made at the top left leaves it out. Switched on, it carries on where it was.
   - **Radar station (R):** a big white dish on a pedestal, turning slowly, on an eight-sided concrete deck with a railing, globe lamps, a small control hut and a yellow cable run, 6 by 6 tiles. It takes one engineer 120 seconds (each one more a fifth as fast again), costing 10,000 supply and 5,000 metal in all as it's built (833 supply and 417 metal every 10 seconds), and has 3,000 health. Press R (or its button), then click where it goes. Once it's up, the eight squares round the start square open: the whole map, 600 by 600 tiles. One is all it takes (another can't be laid), and the map stays open if it's wrecked. There's nothing out there yet.
   - **Vehicle factory (V):** a tall shed of corrugated steel with a low-pitched roof, a door wide enough for a tank (its roller door up in the drum over it), the team's sign beside the door, and a lower concrete workshop along its side with a fuel tank in front and spare road wheels stacked behind, 7 by 5 tiles. It takes one engineer 90 seconds (each one more a fifth as fast again), costing 1,000 supply and 500 metal in all as it's built (111 supply and 56 metal every 10 seconds), and has 500 health. Press V (or its button), then click where it goes. Once it's up, select it: its panel has a **Tank** button (T), which works as the HQ's buttons do (Shift 5, Ctrl 20, right click to take them off the queue). A tank comes out of the door and drives to the factory's rally flag (right click the ground with the factory selected to move it). If something's in the way of the door (a turret laid in front of it), the tank waits inside, and the game says so. Wrecked or taken down, it gives back what's been paid for the tank in training.
   - **When a deposit runs out,** its building stands idle and the game says so. Each deposit takes one building; once that's taken down (Remove), another can go up there. The dead go for these buildings like any other.
   - **Laying rows:** a row lays a turret on every tile; each is paid for as it's built.
   - **Laying:** click a tile, or hold and drag to lay a row (one per tile, no gaps). Turrets laid while the engineers are building join the work they're on; laid while they're doing something else, they take its place. With Shift, they wait their turn, and the turret button stays on for more. A turret or a building can be laid on a trench: once the work starts, the trench there is filled in (anyone in it steps out).
   - **Blueprints:** laid turrets start as blue blueprints that don't take up their tile. Each engineer builds one at a time. A blueprint becomes a real foundation, filling its tile, when an engineer starts on it.
   - **Calling off:** a blueprint lasts only while an engineer has it in his orders, as in Beyond All Reason. Give them another order (without Shift), stop them, or lose them, and the blueprints nobody else means to build go. Laying a turret again on a blueprint calls it off too. A turret already being built stays when they're called away (right click it to go back to it); to get rid of it, take it down with Remove.
5. **Dig trenches.** Select soldiers and press **C** (or click Trench).
   - **Laying:** click a tile, or hold and drag to lay out a trench line. It can turn corners, and it stays joined. Trenches need bare earth, so not the HQ's concrete and not a turret's or a building's tiles (a trench dug first can be built over, though: see 4).
   - **Digging:** trenches are free. One soldier digs a tile in 5 seconds, and each one more on it digs half as fast again as one (two take 3.3 seconds, three 2.5). The soldiers dig one tile at a time, then climb into the free tiles nearest them. Trench blueprints go the same way as turret ones: when the diggers get other orders, or when a trench is laid again on them. Right clicking a trench that's partly dug sends soldiers back to it.
   - **Cover:** right click a finished trench to send any selected units in, one to a tile. Someone standing still in a finished trench is in cover: acid that comes down on him, or a puddle he stands in, does 80% less (the sandbags take it). Trenches give no cover from zombies' blows.
   - **Hit rates:** how often a shot hits what it's aimed at goes by how far off that is. A soldier hits 100% of the time up to 5 tiles, 80% to 20, 60% to 30 and 10% further; a semi-auto turret 100% to 5, 99% to 15, 90% to 20, 80% to 30 and 30% further; a full-auto turret, which shakes as it fires, 90% to 10, 80% to 30, 70% to 40, 20% to 80 and 10% further; a tank's main gun 100% to 30, 80% to 40, 20% further. On the move or in a trench it's the same. A shot that misses goes by the side of its target and flies on, so it may hit another zombie behind or beside it; one that hits nothing kicks up dust where it comes down. A bullet hits the first zombie in its way, which needn't be the one it was aimed at.
   - **Sound:** each soldier's shot cracks, quietly: loudest with the camera zoomed in close, quieter as it zooms out, and very low zoomed all the way out; quieter, too, the further from where the camera looks, and from its side of the screen. (A tank's gun booms, and its shell bursts, louder and heard further; turrets and machine guns have no sound yet.)
   - **Long shots:** past its range, a shooter fires out to its long shots, but out there most of its shots miss (as the hit rates say): turrets out to 1.5 times their range (a semi-auto turret 30, so 45; a full-auto 40, so 60); troops 1.25 times, but a soldier and a tank, as asked, to 30 (range 20) and 40 (range 30). With anything in range, they shoot that first. A selected turret shows its long range as a fainter ring outside its range.
   - **Spit and blows:** a spitter spits every half second, on target 6 times in 10 (off target, it goes by to one side). Its acid flies to where its target stood when it spat, so someone on the move may be gone by the time it lands. On a man it burns 10 to 20 over a second; on a turret or the HQ's walls it does 10 to 15 (it never misses the HQ's walls). A glob that comes down on the ground leaves a green puddle that burns anyone standing in it 2 to 5 health a second (each puddle its own), for 4 seconds. A zombie's blow never misses: 10 to 15 on a man, 5 to 10 on a turret or the HQ.
   - **Levels:** each kill counts, and with enough kills a soldier, a tank or a turret goes up a level. A soldier has 5 levels, at 60, 160, 300 and 500 kills, and each level past the first gives him 2% more hits at every distance, 5 more rounds in his magazine, half a second off changing it, and 5 supply less upkeep (no more range). A tank levels at the same kills; each level takes half a second off loading and 5 supply off its upkeep. A turret, which kills far more, has 10, at 20, 50, 100, 200, 350, 550, 800, 1100 and 1500 kills, and each gives it 1 more tile of range and 2% more hits. Engineers and zombies have no levels. Each level past the first shows as a gold chevron, centred under the health bar, the bar and chevrons sitting just over its head; from level 6 (only turrets get there), a star, with a chevron under it for each level past the sixth. On a portrait, they're under its bar too. A gold chevron rises over one that has just gone up. Hover a soldier's or a tank's portrait, or a selected turret's panel, for its level, kills, range, upkeep, and how often it hits at its range and at its long range.
6. **Tanks.** A tank trains in 20 seconds at a vehicle factory, costing 50 metal every 5 seconds while it trains (200 in all), has 500 health, and uses 100 supply every 30 seconds (hungry, it drives 80% slower and hits 30 in 100 less, as men do). It's an oblong hull on two tracks with a turret, 3.4 tiles long and 2.1 wide, and takes orders as a soldier does: move, line up, patrol, guard, attack (right click a zombie), stop, fire state. It drives at 3 tiles a second and turns on its tracks, slowly, slowing for a turn and turning on the spot for a sharp one; it keeps to ways with room for its hull to turn anywhere (too narrow a gap, and it goes round), and never goes into a trench (it rides over one). Men and the dead are kept off its hull, and the dead come at its sides. In a block or a line, tanks stand far enough apart to turn, with the men round them (or in a line behind). It has three guns, all under its fire state:
   - **Main gun:** an explosive shell every 6 seconds (half a second quicker each level past the first, 4 seconds at level 5), each using 30 bullets. The turret turns to the nearest zombie it can see within 40 tiles (its range is 30, long shots to 40), or to the one it was sent after. On target (100% out to 30 tiles, 80% to 40), the shell bursts on the first zombie in its way: that one takes all of its 100 to 200 damage, and every zombie within 6 tiles of the burst with no wall between takes the same, less toward the edge (a quarter of it at 6 tiles). Off target, it bursts 6.5 to 9 tiles beside its target, out of reach of the blast. Our own take nothing from a blast. A blast is a fireball and a puff of smoke, and leaves a scorch on the ground that fades over 20 seconds. The dead it kills fall away from it.
   - **Two machine guns** on the turret roof, each turning on its own post: each fires 10 to 20 damage a shot every 0.3 seconds, out to the main gun's range (30, long shots to 40), with a semi-auto turret's hit rates, on the two nearest zombies it can see. The commander's stands higher than the loader's, so they pass each other as they turn.
   - **Levels:** as a soldier's, at 60, 160, 300 and 500 kills (the blast's and the machine guns' kills count too); a level takes half a second off loading and 5 supply off its upkeep. A small ring over a tank fills as the next shell loads. Hover its portrait for its level, kills, range, upkeep, and whether its gun is loaded.
   - **Wrecked,** it stops where it is, burnt dark, and stands for 30 seconds (still in everyone's way), then sinks away.
7. **Survive.** A minute in, and every 30 seconds after, a wave comes in from beyond one edge of the open part of the map (so, at first, about 100 tiles from the HQ: the dead take some 3½ minutes to walk in): 50 zombies, then 70, 90 and so on (twenty more each wave), each with somewhere from 20 to 80 health, shambling half a tile a second. They walk at the HQ, and round any junkyard or warehouse in their way. A structure they find on the way (a turret, a building's walls or the HQ's, within 10 tiles and in sight) they go for first, before any man, and they keep to the first one they found till it's down; with none, they turn on any soldier or engineer within 5 tiles. From wave 5, somewhere from 5% to 20% of each wave are **spitters** (taken from its number, not added), with 20 to 40 health and slower still (0.3 tiles a second): a spitter comes within 5 tiles and spits acid, at a structure it has found first (walking up till it's in range), else at the nearest man it can see. Soldiers and turrets shoot anything they can see, out to their long range.
8. **The dead** fall away from the blow that killed them, turning as they go to land on their back (shot from the front) or their face (from behind), arms flung out, each a little differently. Soldiers drop their rifles, and a stain spreads under each body (dark red, dark for zombies, green for a spitter, which bursts in a splash of acid). In a trench the dead lie along it, or sit slumped against its earth wall; by a wall they fall the other way or slump against it. The dead keep clear of each other if they can; where they can't, they pile up, each resting on the bodies under it. Bodies lie for 10 seconds, then fade away (and any lying on them fade with them).

## How it works

### The map

- **Size:** `MAP_SIZE` × `MAP_SIZE` tiles (600 × 600: three by three `SQUARE`s of 200), one world unit each. Only `World.open` (x0, z0, x1, z1) is open: at first the middle square, and once a radar station is up the whole map (`World.open_all`, from `Game.open_map`). Units walk (`neighbours`, `circle_free`, `keep_apart`), things are built (`can_build_turret`) and the camera looks (`RTSCamera.bounds`) only in it; waves come in from just beyond it; the ground outside it is drawn darker (`Renderer.set_open`). The dead may walk anywhere. Tile `(i, j)` covers x in `[i, i+1]` and z in `[j, j+1]`, and the ground is at y = 0. The camera starts on the +Z side looking toward −Z, so "north" is −Z.
- **Blocking:** `World.blocked` is a flat list of booleans. The HQ (5 × 4 tiles), the deposits, and started turrets and buildings block their tiles; `World.fixed` marks just what was there from the start (the HQ and the deposits). Trenches don't: anyone can walk through one.
- **Paths:** `find_path` walks straight if the way is clear, otherwise runs A\* over tiles and then smooths the result. A goal on a blocked tile (in a building or a deposit) becomes the nearest free tile to it (`nearest_free`, ring by ring out). If there's no way there (it's walled in), A\* gives the way to the tile it reached nearest the goal, looking at no more than `FIND_MOST` tiles. (On the first, 96-tile map, where units could walk was a flood fill from the HQ's door, worked out again whenever a tile was blocked; on a map of 360,000 tiles that took too long, so it's gone.)
- **Deposits** (`deposits.py`): `place_deposits` puts each map's junkyards and warehouses at random, as far from the HQ as `PLAN` says, facing it (the side most toward it), with their building spot and `EDGE` tiles of room in the open square, and `APART` tiles clear of the HQ's concrete and of each other. Each is built in its own frame facing +Z and turned on the map (`Deposit.model`); its spot is on whole tiles whichever way it faces, and a building there is turned the same way (`spot_model`), its back to the deposit.
- **The way home:** zombies walk straight at what they're after and slide along walls, with no path finding, so a deposit between them and the HQ would stop them. `World.find_way_home` works out once, for every tile, how far it is to walk to the HQ round the deposits; a zombie that finds a deposit between it and the HQ when it looks round (`Zombie.check_way`, `World.deposit_between`) walks to the next tile on that way (`next_step_home`) till it can walk straight again. Turrets and buildings aren't gone round: the dead go for those.
- **Off the map** there are no walls. Zombies spawn out there and walk in; `hits_wall` ignores off-map space.

### Drawing (`gfx.py`)

- **The ground** is not a mesh of tiles. It's one huge square drawn with `GROUND_FS`, a fragment shader that works out every pixel from its world position with noise: dead grass, dirt, puddles that reflect the sky, burn marks, a rutted track, and the HQ's concrete apron with seams and cracks. Uniforms tell it where the apron and HQ are (`set_hq_ground`) and outline an area (`set_mark`: a selection, or the tile a turret or trench would go on). Outside the map it fades darker into the fog.
- **Trenches** are real holes. `GROUND_FS` throws away the ground's pixels on any tile marked in a small texture (`set_holes`), and `models.build_trenches` builds all the dug tiles into one mesh below the ground: the floor, earth walls on the sides with no trench beside them, and planks and sandbags once a tile is finished. `Game.refresh_trenches` rebuilds that mesh only when `World.trench_changes` counts up, which is at each of a tile's 5 spadefuls.
- **Everything else** is built by `MeshBuilder` as flat-coloured boxes and drawn with `WORLD_FS`: an overcast light, a slight desaturation, distance fog, and a per-draw `tint` (red flash when hit, see-through ghosts and blueprints).
- **Drawing only what shows** (`Game.in_view`): units and the dead off the screen (more than `VIEW_MARGIN` past its edge) aren't drawn, nor their shadows and stains; a body off the screen is still laid out to rest (`lay_out_unseen`), so it's lying there when it comes into view. The living dead on the screen aren't drawn one part at a time: their bodies, legs and arms are gathered (`Game.together`) and each kind of part drawn in one go (`Renderer.draw_many`, with the `MANY_VS` shader, each copy's matrix and tint in a buffer), which is the same picture for far less work. Past `PLAY_DIST` a zombie is drawn as one standing piece (`Game.whole_zombie`). Bodies and limbs are placed with `mathx.standing` and `hung`, the same matrices as the chains they stand for, written out. The overlay goes to the graphics card straight from where pygame keeps it (`upload_overlay`), the shader turning it the right way up.
- **Ghosts:** `draw_ghost` draws a mesh see-through without its back faces showing (depth-only pass first, then colour).
- **The 2D overlay** (panels, bars, text) is drawn with pygame onto a surface every frame and uploaded as a texture (`upload_overlay`), then drawn over the 3D view.

### People

Soldiers, engineers and zombies all share one skeleton:
- **Body:** stands on y = 0 and faces +Z.
- **Legs:** hang from the hips at (±0.1, 0.42).
- **Arms:** hang from the shoulders at (±0.22, 0.80), a little forward of the middle (`models.SHOULDER_X`, `SHOULDER_Z`); a zombie's at (±0.28, 0.80).

`Game.draw_troop` and `draw_zombie` swing those parts with matrices; nothing is skinned. The dead are drawn by `Game.draw_fallen` instead (see below). An engineer carries no gun: his arms hang a little out from his sides (`ARMS_OUT`, so they clear his tool belt) and swing against his legs as he walks. A soldier's rifle is a separate mesh. He holds it two ways (`models.Hold`, `RIFLE_HOLD`), his body always square to the way he faces, walking or firing. Walking or standing about, he carries it across his chest, the muzzle down to his left. To fire, he raises it level in front of him, its sight in front of his right eye and its butt against the right of his chest. Between, as he raises it (his `aim_blend`), the gun turns from across him to forward, rising and coming out a little on the way so its stock swings up over his right forearm and round in front of his chest (`Hold.lift`, `RAISE_OUT`), and his left hand slides along the front (`Game.gun_hold`). His right hand is always on the grip and his left on the front of the gun. A soldier holding his rifle has elbows: each arm is drawn in two pieces (`models.build_arm_halves`), and `Game.arm_joints` rolls his left shoulder forward and in as he raises the gun, reaching across him for its front (`FRONT_SHOULDER`), puts each hand on its grip, and finds where the elbow goes for the hand to reach (`mathx.elbow`; carrying, both elbows bend down; raised, the right one down and out); `mathx.bone` hangs each piece from joint to joint. (The dead's arms stay in one piece.) For that to be reachable, the shoulders sit as a man's do, close to the vest and forward of the middle (`models.SHOULDER_X`, `SHOULDER_Z`). While a soldier digs (or an engineer hammers, his other arm at his side), the rifle is slung diagonally across his back, the muzzle up past his right shoulder, clear of the helmet, resting on his pack or back: `sling_depth` works out from the boxes of the body and the gun how near it can hang without going into him (`SLING_GAP`, 0.5 cm, off him). `Unit.sink` lowers anyone standing in a trench onto its floor. A shot's tracer starts at the rifle's muzzle (`RIFLE_MUZZLE`), placed through the same matrix.

### The dead (`deaths.py`)

- **A blow has a source.** `hurt(damage, source)` takes where the blow came from (the shooter, the zombie, the spitter's mouth). The blow that kills a unit or zombie makes its `Fall`.
- **The ways to go down,** likeliest first (`Fall.options`): away from the blow with arms and legs flung out, then the same tucked in, then turned further and further round, then slumped where it stands. In a trench: along the trench if it runs on far enough, else sitting with its back to the earth wall. Each way keeps off walls, trench holes, other bodies, and anyone standing (or still falling) nearby; in the open, a body falls onto others only if there's no other way.
- **Every pose is worked out from the model,** by `Pose`, the first time the body is drawn (`lay_out`): how far the body tips (a soldier lies propped up on his pack, hips on the ground too), how far down it goes, and the tilt that lays each arm and leg on the ground without going into it. A limb that can't lie without going in lifts the body instead. On the way down, the body is held up by whichever part is lowest, so nothing goes into the ground mid-fall either. Sitting in a trench, the body slides back till its back nearly touches the earth, and spreads its legs till its feet keep off the wall in front.
- **The pose is checked against the map,** corner by corner (`Pose.fits`): no part in a wall, none resting over a trench's hole, and in a trench, nothing below the ground outside it. The first way that fits is taken. The tiles a body lies on are kept in `World.bodies` till it has faded, so others fall clear of it.
- **Bodies stack.** Every body that's laid out marks a map of how high the dead lie (`World.tops`, cells a tenth of a tile across), from points over the faces of the boxes it's made of (`MeshBuilder.boxes`, `deaths.surface`); a dropped gun marks it too. A body that would land on others (`Pose.lands_on_bodies`) rests on top of them instead: it's tilted the way that lets it settle lowest (`settle_on_bodies`, as a dropped weight settles), then lowered till the lowest point of its surfaces touches what's under it (`Ground.clearance`). Its limbs fall as limp limbs do (`Pose.rest`): to the tilt that brings their middle lowest without going into anything, then on to where they touch; one that touches nothing hangs straight down, or is tucked in along the body. A pose that leaves a body balanced on one limb isn't steady, and the next way down is tried (at most `PILE_TRIES` on a pile). A body fades when one it lies on does (`Fall.rests_on`), so nothing is left in the air.
- **Laying out costs time:** about 2 ms on open ground, 10 to 14 on a pile (at worst 20 to 65, by how the pile falls). At most `LAYOUTS_A_FRAME` (1) body is laid out a frame; any others are drawn as they stood for a frame or so more.
- **Rifles** drop from the hands and land on their side beside the body, past its feet or head (in a trench), or across its lap if there's no room on the ground (`Game.gun_spot`).
- **A flaw still to fix:** in a trench, the dead (and their dropped guns) can lie up to 14 cm into its earth walls. A trench's earth walls take up 0.14 of its tile on each side with no trench beyond (`models.RIM`), but `Pose.fits`, `Fall.sit_in_trench` and `Game.gun_spot` treat the whole tile as open.
- **Stains and fading:** a stain spreads under the chest for 3 seconds (`Game.draw_stain`); a body fades away (drawn see-through) over the last 1.5 of its 11.5 seconds (`DEATH_TIME`, `FADE_TIME`).

### Orders (`orders.py`)

- **The queue:** every soldier and engineer has a list, `Trooper.orders`. The first is the one he's on. `Trooper.order(order, queue)` replaces the list, or with `queue` (Shift) adds to its end. An order can take in the next one instead (`take_in`): more turrets laid while engineers build join their `Build`, and a Shift-clicked patrol point joins the `Patrol`.
- **Each frame,** `Trooper.obey` calls the order's `update`, which returns True when it's done; then the next one `begin`s. `obey` gets an `Around` (the zombies, every worker, the turrets and the HQ), because guards and patrols look about them.
- **Patrol stops to shoot:** its `halts` is set, so while the unit is aiming at a zombie in his range (`Gunner.aim_at`, picked in `fight`, and in `Gunner.seen`), `Unit.halted` keeps it from walking on. Long shots he takes on the move.
- **Jobs:** `Build`, `Repair`, and patrols and guards that find work give a worker a `job`. He walks all the way to his own spot beside it (`spot_beside`), then lends it a hand each frame (`Worker.work`, `Site.lend_hand`): building it if it's unfinished, repairing it if it's built, or taking it down on a `Remove` order. After everyone has obeyed, each site does the work of all its hands at once (`Site.do_work`): one alone does `BUILD_TIME`'s worth, and each one more adds `MORE_HANDS` of one (a fifth for engineers on a turret, a half for soldiers on a trench). Engineers build and repair turrets and buildings (`can_work_on`: any `Structure`); soldiers dig trenches. The HQ repairs itself.
- **Blueprints are orders:** `Game.tidy_blueprints`, every frame (paused too), calls off any blueprint no living worker has in a `Build` order or as his job, and marks each turret an engineer has orders to take down (`Site.coming_down`). A turret coming down needs no work, so patrols, guards and `Build` orders leave it alone.
- **Fire states** (`Gunner.fire_state`, soldiers only) only change what a unit picks to shoot by itself. A zombie's `victim` says who it's going for, which is how return fire and guards know.
- **Where people stand:** `spot_round` finds a spot round something, on the unit's side, away from spots others have taken, and clear of walls and of every turret's gun as it turns (`World.guns`, `gun_over`). A unit with no orders left standing where a gun would swing into him steps out of the way (`Trooper.step_clear`), unless he's down in a trench, where the guns pass over his head.
- **Order lines:** `Game.draw_order_lines` draws each selected unit's orders on the ground (`Order.points`), from where he is through every order in his queue.

### One frame (`Game.update`, then `Game.fight`)

1. **Camera:** keys, screen edges and glide.
2. **Ghosts:** while choosing a spot, the turret, trench or building ghost (or a dragged row of them) follows the mouse; a salvage crane or loading dock post snaps to a deposit's spot, and a faint one shows on each free spot of its kind.
3. **`fight`**, unless the game is paused or over; at double speed (`Game.speed`, `FAST`) twice, each a step of the same length, so the game plays out just as it does at normal speed:
   1. **HQ:** it makes materials (`Headquarters.INCOME`: supply, metal and bullets), repairs itself, and trains; a finished unit walks to the rally point. It feeds the troops (`Headquarters.feed`): every `UPKEEP_TIME` it takes each living one's `upkeep` (his kind's `UPKEEP`, `LEVEL_UPKEEP` less a level) from the stores, and marks those it can't pay for `hungry`; a hungry one is fed as soon as there's enough. A hungry trooper's `pace` is `HUNGRY_PACE` of his speed (his legs swing slower to match), and a hungry soldier's or tank's `hit_chance` is `HUNGRY_MISS` less than it would be. The HQ's training is paid for as it goes (`Trainer.train_step`, `stores.owing`): with too little for the next bit, it waits. So is building (`Site.do_work`, `pay_for`), and each vehicle factory's training.
   2. **Waves:** `Waves.update` may bring a new wave.
   3. **Zombies:** each zombie `think`s, given the live units and the *started* turrets (never blueprints). A spitter's `think` may return a new `Spit`. Globs of acid fly on, and those that arrive `land`; one that came down on the ground leaves an `AcidPool`, and every puddle `burn`s whoever stands in it.
   4. **Troops:** each soldier `fight`s and may shoot, and each tank (`Tank.fight`: its main gun's shell, `Game.fire_shell` and `blast`; its machine guns' shots, `Game.fire`); then each trooper `obey`s his orders (a worker lends a hand to his job here). Then every turret and trench does the work of the hands on it (`Site.do_work`). A foundation going down clears anyone off its tile (`World.new_walls`, then `clear_tile`). A soldier who finishes his digging, with no other orders, climbs into the nearest free trench.
   5. **Turrets and buildings:** each turret `fight`s, each building makes what it makes (`Maker.update`), and each vehicle factory trains (`Game.run_vehicle_factory`); a salvage crane or loading dock post takes it out of its deposit (`Extractor.make`), and when that runs out the game says so. A wrecked one frees its tiles (`Structure.hurt`).
   6. **Moving:** everyone `update`s (walks along their path; acid on a trooper burns), then `keep_apart` nudges overlapping people apart (never into walls), and each tank (or its wreck) puts anyone on its hull off it (`Tank.shove`).
   7. **Clean-up:** the dead are removed once they've faded (`DEATH_TIME`), and the tiles they lay on are freed (`World.remove_body`). If the HQ is at 0 health, the game is over.
4. **Hover, markers, tracers, dust, level-up chevrons and the message timer** are updated (tracers, dust and chevrons at the game's speed; markers and messages in real time). (Each soldier's shot in `fight` plays a gunshot, `Game.hear_shot`; at most `sounds.NEW_A_FRAME` start in one frame, at double speed too.)

### Shooting rules (`Gunner.fight`, `Turret.fight`)

- **Range and sight:** a shooter fires at the nearest zombie it can see within its long range (`Fighter.long_range`: its range times `LONG_SHOTS`, which is `TROOP_LONG_SHOTS`, 1.5, for soldiers and `TURRET_LONG_SHOTS`, 2, for turrets). Its range (`Fighter.range`) is its kind's `RANGE`, and `LEVEL_RANGE` (1 tile) more for each level past the first. `Gunner.seen` keeps only the zombies within its range: a soldier sent after a zombie closes in till it's in there (shooting on the way), and a patrol stops only for those. `line_clear` walks the line from tile to tile (`wall_along`), so nobody shoots through the HQ, a factory or a turret, not even past a corner. A turret skips its own tile.
- **Magazines** (`Gunner.fight`): a soldier's rifle holds `MAGAZINE` (30) rounds. Each shot takes one, and when it's empty he changes it, which takes `MAGAZINE_TIME` (5 s) at level 1: he keeps turning to his target, but his gun is down (`aiming` 0) and he can't fire. It's drawn as a change (`Game.reload_pose`, through `RELOAD_STEPS`): his right hand stays on the grip, and his left goes from the front of the gun to the magazine, pulls it down out of the gun, puts it in the pouch at the top of his left vest pocket, draws a full one, brings it up under the gun and seats it, and goes back to the front. For that, his rifle in his hands is drawn without its magazine (`models.build_rifle(with_magazine=False)`) and the magazine on its own (`build_magazine`, its top at `MAG_SEAT`). Over his head, under his rank, a small ring (`Game.reload_ring`, `RELOAD_RING`) fills as the change goes. When there's nothing left in his reach and his magazine isn't full, he changes it then, so the next fight starts with a full one. Each level past the first, his magazine holds `LEVEL_ROUNDS` (5) more and he changes it `LEVEL_CHANGE` (0.5 s) quicker (`Gunner.magazine`, `change_time`). Turrets are fed by belt and never stop. The bullets still come out of the stores a shot at a time; the magazine only sets when he can fire.
- **Bullets:** a soldier or a turret fires only if it can pay `SHOT` (a bullet) from the stores, which `Gunner.fight` and `Turret.fight` are handed. With none left it still turns to its target and aims, but doesn't fire; `Game.update` says so when they run out, and not again till `BULLETS_AGAIN` (100) are back, so a factory's trickle doesn't set it off every 10 seconds.
- **Looking round:** each shooter works out its range once a step (not once a zombie), and looks around every `LOOK_EVERY` (0.2) seconds, staggered, and keeps the list of zombies in sight between looks. Checking line of sight every frame dropped big fights to about 67 frames a second; this keeps them about 100 (about 100 to 106 over waves 4 to 8, with the dead falling and piling up and shooters looking out to their long range, 120 before the dead lay more than 4 seconds). Walking tile to tile, rather than checking every quarter of a tile, made `line_clear` about ten times faster.
- **Full-auto turret:** it's a minigun, whose six barrels take 0.4 seconds to spin up before the first shot, and run down over 1.5 seconds once there's nothing in sight. They're a mesh of their own, turned by `MinigunTurret.barrels`.
- **Hit rates** (`units.Fighter`): each kind's `HITS` lists how often its shots hit at each distance, nearest first: (out to this far, this often). `Fighter.hit_chance` looks the distance up and adds `LEVEL_HITS` for each level past the first (2% for a soldier or a turret), up to 100%. `Game.fire` rolls it: on target, the bullet flies straight at the zombie's middle; off target, it's turned to go by the zombie's side, `MISS_BY` (0.1 to 0.7 tiles) past its edge. Then `Game.bullet` follows it in a straight line: it hits the first living zombie whose body (its `RADIUS`) the line crosses, so a miss may hit one behind or beside the target, and a zombie in front takes a shot meant for one behind it; or it stops at the first wall (`World.wall_along`, which steps from tile to tile), or comes down in the dirt `BULLET_PAST` (0.5 to 3 tiles) beyond what it was aimed at, on a trench's floor if it's over one. Where it stops without hitting anyone, it kicks up dust (`draw_dust`). Each shot does somewhere in its kind's `DAMAGE` (`Fighter.damage`).
- **Spit** (`Spitter.spit_at`, `Spit`): a spitter aims at its target's chest, on target `HIT_CHANCE` (60%) of the time; off target, at a spot `MISS_BY` (0.5 to 1 tile) beside its edge. The glob flies in an arc to that spot and doesn't follow anyone. As it flies it comes down on any of our troops it meets at a height they stand (`Spit.on`), or any turret or HQ wall it runs into (`Spit.wall_at`); meeting nothing, it flies on past the spot, falling, and splashes on the ground. The HQ is always on target: its walls are too big to miss. On a man, the acid burns (`Trooper.burn`): his kind's `DAMAGE` (10 to 20) over `BURN_TIME` (1 s). On a turret or a wall, `WALL_DAMAGE` (10 to 15) at once. In cover in a trench, acid (a glob, or a puddle he stands in) does `COVER_ACID` (20%) of what it would: the sandbags take the rest. A glob that comes down on the ground leaves an `AcidPool` (`POOL_RADIUS`, 0.5 tiles), which burns anyone whose feet are in it and at its level somewhere in `POOL_DAMAGE` (2 to 5) health a second (each puddle its own) for `POOL_TIME` (4) seconds. It spreads only as far as it can without running into a wall, over a trench's hole, or (on a trench's floor) into the earth walls; with no room even for 0.1, there's no puddle.
- **What the dead go for first** (`Zombie.find_structure`): every `LOOK_EVERY` seconds a zombie with no structure looks for the nearest started turret, or wall of a bullet factory or the HQ, within `STRUCTURE_NOTICE` (10 tiles) that it can see (`Zombie.sight_of`: to a turret, `line_clear` skipping its own tile; to walls, to just short of their nearest point). Once it has found one (`Zombie.found`), it goes for that before anything else, and keeps to it till it's down, even if another comes nearer. At a factory or the HQ (`walled`) it goes for its own spot along the wall it's off (`Zombie.wall_spot`: the nearest point of that wall, moved along it by the zombie's `offset`, so the dead spread along it), and walks right up against the wall's tiles before it strikes (`WALL_REACH`, 0.1 past its edge: the tiles are blocked, so that's as near as it gets). A factory's hall and tanks fill its tiles to within a third of a tile of their edge, so where the dead stop is at its walls. (The spot along a wall used to be moved both ways, along and in, so near the wall it could end up inside the building, and the zombie pushed at the wall without ever striking; some of those at the HQ's south and east walls did.) A spitter, which spits only 5 tiles, walks up to it till it's in range. With none, a zombie turns on a man within `NOTICE` (5), else walks at the HQ; a spitter spits at the nearest man within its range that it can see, else walks at the HQ.
- **Tanks** (`Tank.fight`): every `LOOK_EVERY` a tank sorts the living dead within its long range by distance and checks its line of fire to them nearest first (at most `LOOK_AT_MOST`, 40), picking what its main gun goes for and the two nearest for its machine guns. The turret turns at `TURRET_TURN` and fires when it's on target; it won't turn its barrel into a wall (`barrel_clear`: it passes over turrets, being higher than their guns), and goes the long way round instead. `Game.fire_shell` rolls the hit as `Game.fire` does, but an off-target shell is aimed `Tank.MISS_BY` (3.5 to 6 tiles) beside its target; `Game.bullet` finds where it bursts, and `Game.blast` does the damage (`Tank.blast_damage`). Each shell puts its 30 bullets on what's been fired, for the numbers by the materials.
- **Blows:** a zombie's blow never misses: `DAMAGE` (10 to 15) on a man, `WALL_DAMAGE` (5 to 10) on a turret or the HQ.
- **Walking:** a zombie's legs swing as fast as it goes, a stride (`Zombie.STEP`, from how far its legs swing) for every half swing, so its feet don't slide.
- **Kills and levels:** the kill goes to whichever soldier or turret fired the shot (`Fighter.count_kill`, from `Game.fire`). One that goes up a level is put on `World.promoted`, and `Game.update` moves it to `levels_up`, which draws the rising chevron. The dead don't count kills.
- **Holding still:** units **never walk off after zombies by themselves**. They turn and shoot whatever's in reach (as far as their fire state lets them), even while walking. Only orders send them after one: a right click on a zombie, or a guard going after a zombie that's going for what he guards. Either way they stop once it's in range and in sight.

## Testing

There are no unit-test files. The game is tested by playing it headless and by looking at pictures.

- **The scripted run:** `python Skyline/main.py --screenshot out.png` trains units, lays a turret, waits for a wave, and saves `out_hq.png`, `out_build.png` and `out.png`. It prints a one-line summary.
- **Your own checks:** write a script that makes `main.Game(screenshot="x")` (so the mouse isn't read; it opens a 1280 × 720 window, or another size with `window=(1920, 1080)`, or the whole screen with `window=None`), then calls its methods: `select_hq` + `train(Soldier)` (or `train(Soldier, 5)`, and `untrain`), `toggle_pause`, `toggle_speed`, `select_units`, `command_move`, `command_line`, `command_attack`, `command_patrol`, `command_on` (guard, build or repair), `command_remove`, `command_stop`, `cycle_fire_state`, `set_group` / `recall_group`, `select_kind_on_screen`, `start_placing_turret` + `aim_turret` + `place_turret`, `start_placing_building(kind)` + `aim_building` + `place_building` (a bullet factory, a salvage crane or a loading dock post), `select_deposit`, `start_turret_row` / `extend_turret_row` / `finish_turret_row`, `start_placing_trench` + `start_trench_row` / `extend_trench_row` / `finish_trench_row`. The `command_*`, `place_turret` and `finish_*_row` methods take `queue=True` for Shift. A unit can also be given an order straight: `unit.order(Move(x, z))`. Step `update(1 / 60, edge_scroll=False)` in a loop and assert on the state. Without the `screenshot` argument, real input works too: post pygame mouse and key events and call `handle_events`. Mock `pygame.mouse.get_pos` if you need hover (such as a button's details) and drag previews, because the hidden test window gets no real mouse movement. `pygame.key.set_mods` stands in for holding Shift or Ctrl. Fights are random (which shots hit, damage, waves, how the dead fall): `random.seed` makes a run repeat. Setting `HITS = ((math.inf, 1.0),)` on a shooter makes every shot go where it aims (and `0.0`, every shot miss); measure hit rates by firing many shots with `Game.fire` and counting the damage done. `give_test_gift` adds materials, as the + button does; a check that trains, builds or shoots a lot needs it. A soldier's or a turret's `fight` takes the stores to pay its bullets from (`game.base.stores`).
- **Speed:** there's no frame counter on screen. Time a few hundred `update` + `draw` + `display.flip` calls with many units. Your monitor's refresh rate (vsync) caps the result.
- **Deposits:** `game.deposits` are where they fell this game (`random.seed` before making the game picks the map); a check that needs ground clear of them can look at their `rect`s. `Game.crane_pose(crane)` gives where a crane's magnet is for its `making`, and `models.crane_arm` where its arm is.
- **Changing magazines:** `Game.reload_pose(soldier)` gives where his left hand is and the magazine's matrix (None while it's in his pouch) for his `changing`, in the frame of the way he faces, and `arm_joints` the whole arm.
- **Look at the pictures** after any change to models or the ground. Parts must make physical sense: nothing floating, overlapping or poking through.
- **Guns in hand:** for a soldier at the origin, give `Game.gun_matrix` and `Game.arm_joints` the identity as his frame (so they're in the models' own units), carried, at every tenth of the way to raised, raised, and slung. Check that no point all through each box of the gun (`MeshBuilder.boxes`), nor round each arm, is inside a box of the body or of a leg swung as far as it goes walking; that the gun doesn't go through an arm; that each hand reaches its grip with the elbow bent; and that the muzzle is out in front. Allowed: 1 cm of a butt pressed to the chest or an arm against the side, an arm where it joins the shoulder, the hands on the gun, and the top of the shooting arm, where the butt rests. For an engineer, check his arms, hanging and swung as far as they go walking, against his body's boxes and his legs swung the other way.
- **The dead:** `Game.fallen_parts(unit, shape, arm_start)` gives each part's matrix at the unit's `dead_for`; apply them to the `Shape`'s corners to check, at the end and on the way down, that nothing goes into the ground, a wall or the earth round a trench, and that every part rests on the ground. For piles, kill many in a small space one after another and check box by box (`Shape.boxes`) that no body goes into another, and that each rests on the ground or on bodies laid out before it.

## Decisions so far (and why)

- **No assets, all code.** Meshes are boxes built in `models.py`, and the ground is a shader, so the game is a handful of `.py` files.
- **The dead lie where they physically can** (on request, for more detail when troops and zombies die). Rather than one canned fall, each pose is worked out from the model and checked against the map, so a body never ends up in a wall, in the earth, over a hole, or in another body: where there's no room, it rests on top of the others (on request, "bodies stack, not overlap"). The models have no knees or elbows, so the dead lie or sit with straight limbs. A dark stain is all the gore there is.
- **The ground is one shader, not tiles.** Tile-coloured ground looked blotchy. The shader is sharp at every zoom and costs little.
- **The map was once a floating island** with trees and rocks. Those were removed on request ("just ground"), and the style changed from medieval to a zombie apocalypse.
- **The commander** (a unit in black tactical gear) was removed from the game while the map was improved. Its model and code are in git history, in the first Skyline commit (`8be9aa5`).
- **Units hold still.** An earlier "guard your post and chase" behaviour was removed on request: units only move when ordered. Patrol and guard are orders, given by the player.
- **Orders work as in Beyond All Reason** (on request): a queue with Shift, patrol, guard, repair, stop, fire states, control groups, and workers on patrol taking on work nearby. The names, look and keys are Skyline's own. BAR's move states (roam and so on) and its wait order were left out: units here hold still unless ordered.
- **Laying turrets joins the work in hand,** even without Shift, as it did before there were orders, so laying one turret after another gets them all built.
- **Workers walk all the way to their spot** before they start work, so they stand close by what they work on, not back at arm's length.
- **Nobody stands in a gun's sweep.** A turret's gun reaches out past its tile as it turns, so workers and guards stand clear of it, and anyone left standing in it steps out.
- **Turrets are blueprints first,** so laying out a long row doesn't wall in your own units before anyone builds it.
- **Blueprints go with the orders behind them** (on request, as in Beyond All Reason): one nobody means to build is called off, so stopping or re-ordering builders clears what they hadn't started. What's started stays, and only engineers can take it down (`Remove`), building in reverse. A blueprint called off gives back all it cost; a turret taken down gives nothing back.
- **Spitters come close** (on request). They were first asked for as ranged zombies that hit like a soldier, with its damage, rate of fire and range (20). That was cut down on request to a range of 6 and 6 damage a glob, with a glob that misses leaving a burning puddle; and then, with the new stats, to a range of 5, on target 6 times in 10, but a glob every half second that burns a man for a second.
- **The dead go for structures first** (on request): a turret, a bullet factory or the HQ they find within 10 tiles (first 6), before any man, keeping to the first they found. Since spitters find structures further off than they spit, they walk up to one till it's in range. Turrets draw the dead to them and soak up their blows. This also fixed spitters never spitting at turrets: their line of sight to a turret ended inside the turret's own tile, which counted as a wall.
- **Soldiers carry a modern rifle** (on request), in black: an adjustable stock on a buffer tube, a pistol grip, a curved magazine, a railed handguard round a short barrel, a gas block and flash hider, and a red-dot sight on the top rail. It's a generic one, not any real model. It replaced, in turn, a short dark slab that read as a submachine gun, and then a wooden bolt-action rifle. Its stock is slid in short, as a soldier wearing a vest would have it.
- **Guns held like a rifleman's** (on request: the old hold looked wrong). Before, both hands met at the grip with straight arms, the gun pointing down in front of him. Now he holds it up to fire with a hand on the grip and one on the front, his elbows bent. Straight arms couldn't reach that: the models' shoulders were wide (their arms 4.5 cm off the vest) and set back in the middle of a deep chest. So soldiers and engineers were made slimmer (the vest 38 cm across, now 32), their shoulders brought in to the vest and forward to where a man's are, and their living arms given elbows. The hold itself was found by searching for one where both hands reach with the elbows bent and nothing goes into the body, the legs or the other arm. At first he stood side-on all the time, so he walked crabwise; on request he then walked square, carrying the gun across his chest, and turned side-on only to fire; and on request again he now fires square too, as soldiers do with a modern rifle. Square, his left hand only reaches the front of the gun with the gun in front of the right of his chest (not out at his shoulder: the models' shoulders are broad for their arms) and his left shoulder rolling forward and in. Engineers held a shotgun the same way, until on request they stopped carrying a gun.
- **Rank goes under the health bar** (on request), centred, with the bar lifted to make room so neither covers the head.
- **Soldiers wear a plain uniform** (on request): olive fatigues, a khaki vest and an olive helmet, with none of the team's blue. Engineers still have the blue band on their hard hat and their armbands.
- **One unit to a trench tile.** A tile is only as wide as one person with room to stand clear of the walls, so orders spread units along the trench, and anyone left over waits beside it.
- **The HQ starts in the middle of the map.** The player used to choose its spot, and that step was removed on request. There is one HQ; other teams aren't in the code yet.
- **A clean screen.** Range rings show only for a selected turret and one being laid (its range, and fainter, its long range), and there's no frame counter. The always-on range rings (V to hide) and the frame counter were removed on request.
- **No how-to text on screen** (on request): no hint at the start, and no banner while laying turrets or trenches or choosing where to patrol (the ghosts, and the lit Patrol button, show what's going on). The banner under the clock only gives news: a wave coming in, a group made, or a tile that won't take a turret or trench.
- **Panels are buttons and bars, with no labels or hints** (on request). The HQ's panel has its name, its health, a button for each kind it trains, and under them what it makes; a building's, its name, its health and what it makes (and beside a deposit, what's left in it); a deposit's, its name and a bar of what's left in it. What's made shows as each material's mark and how much how often ("+200/10s"), with no words (`Game.draw_income`). The units' panel has their portraits, a row of order buttons and a row of build buttons. A turret's panel has its name, its health, and a blue bar of how far it's built. What a button makes (its damage a shot and a second, range, health, time to train or build, and cost) shows only while the mouse is on it (`Game.about`, `draw_tooltip`). Levels show as chevrons (and from level 6, a star) under the health bars, with the numbers (level, kills, the next level, range, how often it hits at its range and long range) only on hover, over a soldier's portrait or a turret's panel (`Game.record`). The materials, at the top left, show what's made in all only on hover. The Idle button, at the bottom right, has a title ("Idle") so it's clear what the portrait there means, and shows only while there's an idle engineer.
- **Hit rates by distance, set by hand** (on request, with the new stats). The first try gave every attack a flat 3 in 10 chance to miss, at any distance; that looked wrong, since a soldier missed a zombie at arm's length as often as one across the map. Then every shot strayed a little off its aim by a bell curve, so how often it hit fell off smoothly with distance (and on the move, and braced in a trench). Now each kind has a table of hit rates by distance, as asked, so the numbers are exactly what was set; moving or bracing no longer changes them. A miss still looks like one: it goes by the side of its target and flies on, may hit another zombie behind or beside it, and kicks up dust where it comes down. Spit doesn't follow its target, so moving is the way to dodge it. Each kind levels at its own pace, because a turret kills dozens of zombies a game and a soldier a handful; the dead don't level any more. There's no friendly fire: bullets pass by our own units. Spit comes down on any of ours in its way.
- **Long shots** (on request): soldiers open fire past their range, out to 1.5 times it, and turrets (with the new stats) out to twice theirs, but there most of their shots miss. Spitters weren't part of the request, so they spit only within their range.
- **No fight order.** An order to go somewhere stopping to shoot on the way was taken out on request; patrol does that.
- **Orders can be given while paused,** as in Beyond All Reason, so a pause is a time to plan. The Pause key was picked because P is patrol; the button at the top right is there for keyboards without one.
- **Materials** (on request): supply trains troops, metal builds turrets and bullet factories, and bullets are what the guns fire. The third material was electric at first (the HQ made 200 a second, for turrets and buildings later to need); on request it became bullets, which every shot uses, so the guns fall silent if the HQ and the factories can't keep up. At first the HQ made no bullets and a factory 30 every 10 seconds; on request the HQ then made 50 and a factory 100, and now the HQ makes 100 (and 200 supply every 10 seconds, rather than 100 every 30). Units and blueprints are paid for when they're queued or laid, so what's on the screen is what you have left; and calling them off gives it back, so nothing is lost by changing your mind. The + button is there to test with.
- **Engineers carry no gun** (on request): they build and repair. They have no fire state, aren't sent after zombies, and are drawn with their arms at their sides.
- **Crews add up, but less with each man** (on request): one more soldier on a trench adds half of one's pace, and one more engineer on a turret a fifth, so a crew is faster but not by as much again for every man. The site adds up the hands on it each frame (`Site.do_work`), so it's the same however the workers come and go.
- **The HQ repairs itself** (on request), rather than engineers repairing it.
- **The HQ looks like a fortified block** (on request, after a picture of a real headquarters building, with its name and emblem left off): four storeys of dark concrete with long bands of windows lit inside, piers between the bays with lamps, a taller stair tower, a block over the entrance on two piers, a railing round the roof, and a low wall round it at the edge of its tiles with barbed wire on posts (boxes can only turn about the upright, so there's no coiled razor wire), the gate slid open. It's taller than the one-storey bunker it replaced (its walls stand 3.1 high), with storeys lower than a real building's so it doesn't tower over the map. Its wall stands at the edge of its tiles, so the dead and workers stopped there are at it; the old bunker's walls were set back on its slab. The team's colour is on the flag on its roof.
- **The bullet factory** (on request: a building, smaller than the HQ, that engineers build and that makes bullets: first 30 every 10 seconds, now 100) is a design of its own, from a picture of a factory given as the idea: a brick works with chimneys, tanks and a loading door, built from boxes. Its health (1,500) and size (4 by 3 tiles) weren't given, and were picked to start with; so were its build time (90 s) and cost (300 metal and 100 supply), and on request they're now 40 s and 200 metal. It was first drawn with a yard round its hall, and the dead clawed at the air a tile short of its walls; now its hall and tanks fill its tiles.
- **Troops eat** (on request: first 100 supply every 30 seconds each, now 50, and hungry troops walk at half speed and miss more at every range). How much more they miss wasn't given: their hits are halved, so a soldier who'd hit every time at 5 tiles hits half the time. Hunger was picked from a few ideas (losing health, deserting, no more training) because it hurts without losing you the army to one bad minute. They're fed oldest first, so with too little supply the newest go hungry, and one is fed the moment there's enough for him rather than at the next feeding, so the supply a dock post brings in helps at once. The made and used numbers by the materials are every 30 seconds (the upkeep's time), so they're whole numbers for every rate in the game.
- **Soldiers change magazines** (on request: 30 rounds, first 3 seconds to change, now 2; turrets don't). At 10 shots a second that's 3 seconds of fire and 2 changing, so in a long fight a soldier fires about 60% as much as before. The change is animated (on request), with the magazine a part of its own: out of the gun, into his pouch, a full one out, into the gun. His hand holds a magazine by its middle at the gun and by its top at the pouch, so his hand stays out of his vest; it's pulled only 5 cm clear of the gun (the rifle has no magazine well to clear), so his left arm can reach it with the elbow still bent. A check walks the change through 200 steps against his body and the rifle. A small ring over his head (on request, under his rank and smaller than a material's mark) shows how far along the change is. Changing a part-used magazine once the fight's over wasn't asked for; it was added so a soldier doesn't go into the next fight with a few rounds left and stop at once. While he changes he lowers his gun to the carry, which is how you can tell.
- **Bullets are made from supply and metal, and there's a most** (on request: a factory makes 200 every 10 seconds, first 100, but turns 50 supply and 10 metal into them; bullets stop at 10,000, and then the factories stop, using nothing). A factory short of supply or metal waits too, rather than making what it can, and its batch stands still while it waits, so it never uses half a batch. The stores cap bullets for whatever brings them in (the HQ, the test button), not only the factories, so there's never more than 10,000.
- **Faster with hundreds of the dead about** (on request: the frame rate fell too far by waves 4 and 5). With 400 of the dead on the map, a frame took 35 ms (28 a second), most of it drawing: five draws a zombie, each with its matrices worked out in Python, for zombies mostly still walking in off the screen. Now those off the screen aren't drawn, those on it are drawn together by part, their matrices are written out rather than multiplied, `mathx.mul` is unrolled, and shooters and zombies don't work out the same thing over and over (a check draws a crowd both ways and they're the same to the pixel). Drawing 400 took 31.8 ms a frame as before and 13.3 now, in the same run; timings on this machine move a lot from run to run with other programs running, so the checks compare the two ways in one run rather than against a number.
- **Buildings switch on and off** (on request: an on/off button for every factory). It's on the bullet factory, the salvage crane and the loading dock post (all that make something), not the radar station, which only opens the map.
- **Full screen** (on request). The game opens over the whole screen at its own size, as a window with no border. pygame's `FULLSCREEN`, tried first, takes the screen over instead, and Alt+Tab then hides the game. What sits against the right and bottom edges (the pause and double-speed buttons, the Idle button) is placed once the window is open (`lay_out_screen`); everything else was already laid out from the window's size as it's drawn. The panels and buttons keep their size in pixels rather than growing with the screen. The overlay, as big as the screen, is kept from frame to frame and cleared, rather than made anew each frame: at 2560 × 1440 a new one took 4.4 ms a frame, and kept, 1.5. With 400 of the dead on the screen, a frame drew in 6.4 ms at 2560 × 1440.
- **Tanks and a vehicle factory** (on request: a vehicle factory engineers build for 500 metal and 1,000 supply, that trains a tank for 200 metal; the tank with a main gun firing explosive shells (50 bullets a shell, 100 to 200 damage, 8 s to load, half a second less a level as a soldier's change, range 100 and 150 at most, hits 100% to 70, 90% to 100, 80% to 150 and 40% further) and two machine guns that work as a semi-auto turret, and 100 supply upkeep). The 40% past 150 never comes up, as 150 is as far as it fires. Not given, and picked: the factory's size (7 by 5), health (2,500) and build time (90 s); the tank's health (2,000), training time (45 s), speed, size and turning; how far the blast reaches (3 tiles) and how it falls off (to a quarter at its edge), and that a shell off target bursts far enough aside to miss its target; that it levels at a soldier's kills, and a level does nothing but load quicker; and that the machine guns keep a semi-auto turret's first-level numbers. The tank is sized against the men (its turret roof a little over their heads), smaller than a real one is against the buildings, as everything is. Its barrel is over everyone's head and a turret's gun, and its turret stays over its hull as it turns, so nothing it turns swings through anyone; it needs room to turn on the spot all along its way, so it goes round gaps a man would take. A wreck stands 30 seconds, longer than the dead, and sinks away as a wrecked turret does. The dead it kills by its blast fall away from the blast.
- **The balance redone** (on request, by the numbers given for each thing: how the game plays, above, is as it is now; the other entries here give the numbers as they were then). Costs given as so much "every 10 s" ("if building stops, this will not cost") were taken as paid while it's built, as in Beyond All Reason, and the same for training, so nothing is paid at laying or queuing and a stall costs nothing; the radar station and the vehicle factory, whose costs weren't changed, are paid the same way, all of their old cost over their build time. The rule "troops and tank fire out 1.25x" and the soldier's range "20-30" and the tank's "30-40" don't agree: the ranges given for each were taken (so 1.25 is for any troop with none given; there's none yet), and asked about. The shadows under units went, to save a draw each.
- **Zooming out over the whole map** (on request). Up to 72, the old furthest, the camera is as it was; past that it tips over to look nearly straight down, the haze thins and it draws further, out to 800, where the whole 600-tile map fits on the screen. - **Big waves, and pushing crowds apart** (on request: 50 zombies in the first wave, 20 more each wave, first 10 and 5). With the dead walking in for minutes, hundreds of them are about at once, and `keep_apart`, which checked every pair, took 41 ms a frame with 800 of them; it checks only those on neighbouring tiles now (4.4 ms), and a check shows it moves everyone just as before.

- **Junkyards and warehouses** (on request: supply and metal at random spots on the map, that engineers build at to get them out; the junkyard and warehouse were picked from a list of ideas, with what's built at each). - **Double speed** (on request, for testing) runs the game's step twice a frame rather than one step twice as long, so shots, walking and the dead's falls work out exactly as they would at normal speed: a game at double speed is the same game in half the time. F was free (and stands for fast).
